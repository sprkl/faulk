* **Update** (#402): the engine's output goes through an always-on, invisible safety limiter (`engine::limiter`, last stage of `Engine::render`): ceiling -0.3 dBFS, stereo-linked, immediate attack on the sample that goes over (no look-ahead, so 0 frames of added latency — proven at the stage level, the over is caught on its own sample and nothing is shifted, and backed at the engine level by two independent rigs whose onset frame matches, which shows the render topology buffers nothing), exponential release of time constant 80 ms that snaps to unity (a signal under the ceiling passes bit for bit; the gain is back to unity shortly after, not 80 ms after the crête). It sits after the output's meter: the master meter keeps showing the level before the protection, so an overload stays visible; the speakers, the helper's callback and the WAV export (same `Engine::render`) get the protected signal in every format (floats included, so no format clips and the clipped counter stays 0). No command, message or protocol change, and no way to bypass it. A non-finite sample leaves as silence. The gain is not reset on a project swap (retiring tracks keep their gain, no click); it resets after an all-sound-off, and follows `Engine::set_sample_rate` (the keep coefficient is capped below 1.0 so the release always makes progress, at any rate `SampleRate` accepts). Cost at 48 kHz, 128-frame block: 197 ns at rest, 755 ns while limiting (0.007 % and 0.028 % of the budget).

The limiter tracks its peak gain reduction (`Limiter::max_reduction`, one `max` on the audio path, reset per render by `Engine::reset_limiter_reduction`); `Offline::reduction` and `fk_export_stat(…, 7)` expose it; the export dialog shows a reworded warning ("your mix was pushed into the output limiter, up to {db} dB of gain reduction", EN and FR) above `LIMITER_PUSHED_DB` (1 dB), replacing the now-unreachable "clipped" warning and its dead 32-bit-float advice. The file still never clips, so `clipped` stays 0 and is no longer surfaced. Covered native and WASM: a +24 dB mix reports a real reduction, a mix under the ceiling reports exactly 0.
