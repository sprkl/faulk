* **Feature** (#491): a clip moves to another track. New command `arrangement.move_to_track { moves: [{ placement, track }], by? }` (edit, undo through `arrangement.restore`, one history entry): each placement goes to its own MIDI target track (so a selection of several tracks moves one track down together), optionally shifted in time. The clip goes with it as a copy in the target (one copy per source clip and target, so linked placements moved together stay linked; a placement left alone on a shared clip no longer shares it), and a source clip left with no placement and no grid slot is removed; the moved placements get new identifiers in another track and win on overlap there. All or none: an audio target or source, an unknown track, a doubled placement and the limits are refused before anything is written; nothing moving makes no history entry. `PROTOCOL_VERSION` 18. Web: Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the selection one track (the previous or next track, lanes and the master do not count; the first or last track and a non-MIDI track are refused out loud), the mouse drag follows the pointer from row to row (Alt+drag still copies on the clip's own track), and the clip's right-click menu has "Move to the track above" and "below". Tests: command (semantics, grid clip kept, carve, refusals, no-op, exact undo), contract examples, engine messages, the pure helpers, and an end-to-end spec (keyboard, menu, mouse, edge). Judge Dredd's plan review settled: the "row" is the neighbouring track in track order; a conservative limit check (the copy is counted before the emptied source clip is removed).
