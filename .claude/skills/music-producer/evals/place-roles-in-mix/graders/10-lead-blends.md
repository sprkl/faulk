Every `Mix` line gives a level (a fader value in dB), and the lead blends into the track instead of sitting on top of it: its fader starts 2 to 4 dB below the bass's (a starting point: a sine lead may end 1 to 2 dB below it once the render is measured, because the lead check wins), never level with it before any measure; it is not dry: it has a Reverb in the middle plane's room (the same size and damping as the middle plane's Reverbs) with a `mix` of about 0.15 to 0.25 and a predelay of about 0.03 to 0.05 s, and a synced Echo with a `mix` of about 0.15 to 0.25; a square or saw lead also has a lowpass Filter between about 5 and 8 kHz; and the sheet asks for the lead check on the render (the RMS of the densest section with and without the lead, more than about 4 dB meaning the lead is too loud) or writes `to measure on the render`; a lead-check figure given without any render is a fail.

The lead check also holds for every lead and for an opening filter: the sheet has one lead-check line per lead (never only the last lead), and each says the bars measured; when a lead's cutoff, resonance or drive is automated, its check is measured on the bars at the end of the opening, not on the whole section; the lead check wins over the fader range (a lead whose mute gap on the render is outside 1 to 3 dB is moved, whatever its fader says); and when two leads play together a single export gives one gap for both, which the sheet says rather than attributing it to one lead; and a rich (saw or square) lead whose cutoff opens by more than an octave has a matching volume dip of 2 to 4 dB over the same bars, or a bounded opening, written on its `Mix` line. A sheet with one lead-check line for several leads, or an opening of more than an octave with no compensation, is a fail.
