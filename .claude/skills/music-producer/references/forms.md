# Forms

The shapes a track can take, each with a bar map, and the rule that keeps two tracks in a row from taking the same one. [arrangement.md](arrangement.md) turns the chosen shape into seams, events and an ending.

Most habits lead to one shape, here called F0: a layered intro, one part entering at a time, a break, the densest section around two thirds of the way, an exit by subtraction. It works, and because it is the habit it is the one to ration. The others build contrast from silence, register, rhythm, harmony and timbre rather than from the number of parts.

Bar maps assume 4/4 and a 64–96-bar track; scale them to the length the brief sets (the brief's length wins over a map). Sections stay multiples of 4 unless the irregularity is the idea, and then it is named in the sheet.

## The forms

### F0 Ladder
Intro 8–16 (one or two parts), A 16 (parts enter one by one), break 8 (drums out), peak 16 at 55–70 %, exit 16 (parts leave in reverse order). The idea: the track is a staircase of density. Suits: almost anything, which is the problem; ration it.

### F1 Reverse arc (cold open at the peak), 80 bars
1–16 peak: everything, the hook, loudest · 17–24 strip: drums out, chords and hook alone, filter closing · 25–40 regrowth: bass and pulse return with one new counter-line · 41–56 second peak, altered: the same hook over a new chord picture, or half the parts and one new sound · 57–72 dissolve: parts leave in an order different from their entry · 73–80 last voice: the hook on a soft sound, ending on a held or unresolved chord.
Idea: the first bar is the thesis, the rest comments on it; with nothing to wait for, the ear follows change. Suits: euphoric, anthemic; also nostalgic (the memory first, then it fades).

### F2 Silence-hinged drop, 72 bars
1–16 verse: sparse pad, a plucked or chopped phrase, light pulse · 17–24 pre: the filter opens, the phrase repeats shorter (8, 4, 2, 1 beats) · 25 void: one bar of nothing but a tail, or one clean note · 26–41 drop: a short rhythmic hook, hard kick and bass, few notes and a lot of space · 42–49 verse 2: the hook on another instrument, drums halved · 50–57 void 2 and a shorter drop with a mutated hook · 58–72 outro: the hook alone, then a tail.
Idea: the impact comes from the bar before; the drop has fewer parts than the verse. Suits: bright, playful, bass-leaning.

### F3 Single long crescendo, 96 bars
1–32 seed: one ostinato, very quiet · 33–64 accretion by transformation, not by parts: the filter opens in time, octave doubling, a swelling pad, the rhythm subdivides (quarters, eighths, sixteenths) · 65–88 climax plateau: the ostinato at full register and densest rhythm · 89–96 cut: a hard stop on beat 1, or one held chord.
Idea: the same notes get bigger; no break, no dip. Suits: cinematic, tense, awe.

### F4 Theme and variations, 96 bars
1–16 theme, plain · 17–32 variation 1: new rhythmic feel (swing, or half-time drums) · 33–48 variation 2: the same chords, the melody inverted or moved to the bass · 49–64 variation 3: a new key centre (a third up), the theme in canon with itself two bars late · 65–80 variation 4: the theme reduced to its skeleton notes, a new texture around it · 81–96 return: the theme as in bars 1–16 with one detail changed.
Idea: each variation changes one parameter (rhythm, harmony, register, texture, key); the plain return is heard differently. Suits: melancholic, slow-burning, any tempo.

### F5 Two tracks in one, 80 bars
1–32 track 1: its own tempo feel, mode and palette, a small arc and an ending that almost stops · 33–36 hinge: one shared chord or sound holds while everything else is swapped · 37–80 track 2: another tempo family (half or double feel), another key (the relative, or a distant one), another palette; a melodic cell of track 1 returns transformed.
Idea: the hinge holds one common note or chord, so the swap is a door, not a cut. Suits: dark to bright, or the reverse. Needs two palettes, chosen in Frame.

### F6 Loop with one interruption, 64 bars
1–28 a four-bar loop that only changes in timbre (filter, detune, noise level) · 29–32 rupture: the loop cut mid-bar; four bars of something unrelated (a free-time phrase, noise, or silence and one chord) · 33–60 the loop returns re-harmonised, or with one part transposed, and now evolves further · 61–64 it stops mid-phrase.
Idea: everything is expectation; the interruption is the only event and must be bigger than anything in the loop. Suits: hypnotic, minimal, dark or dreamlike.

### F7 Call and response between sections, 64 bars
1–8 call A: chords and lead, a question that ends off the tonic · 9–16 response A: drums and bass answer in rhythm, no lead, ending on the tonic · 17–24 call B: the question higher and louder · 25–32 response B: longer, with a first counter-line · 33–48 merge: calls and responses interleave every two bars, a hocket between lead and bass · 49–64 resolution: both together on the tonic, then thinning.
Idea: sections are speakers, not density levels. Suits: playful, rhythmic, melodic.

### F8 A B A' with a B in another meter, 72 bars
1–24 A: 4/4 groove, three parts · 25–48 B: 6/8 or 5/4, harmony moved to the relative key, the melody in long notes, drums reduced to a pulse (name the irregular bars) · 49–72 A': 4/4 again, with B's melodic cell over A's groove.
Idea: the meter change is a second world; the return takes its drama from the visit. Suits: reflective, wistful, progressive.

### F9 False ending, then a second life, 88 bars
1–40 a short ladder: quick build, peak around bar 28, dissolved to one note by bar 40 · 41–44 tail and near silence: the track has "ended" · 45–60 restart in a half-time feel, the last note as a pedal; drums first, bass later · 61–84 the real peak, in a new key (a fourth up), the first melody now in the bass · 85–88 cut.
Idea: the first ending is believable; the real peak is the reward. Suits: euphoric, cathartic.

### F10 Rondo (A B A C A), 80 bars
1–16 A: the home groove and hook · 17–24 B: new key and sound set, no drums · 25–40 A: the hook an octave up · 41–48 C: rhythmic tension, syncopated, no bass, three against four · 49–72 A at full strength, B's sound as a counter-line, C's rhythm in the hats · 73–80 coda: A's hook alone.
Idea: A never changes its material but borrows from each visit. Suits: upbeat, song-like instrumentals.

### F11 Through-composed morph, 96 bars
1–12 S1: a chord loop over a slow pulse · 13–28 S2: the loop shrinks to a bass cell, a new melody enters · 29–48 S3: the melody becomes the harmony; the drums change feel (swing to straight) · 49–72 S4: the harmony moves; a third idea grows from S3's rhythm · 73–96 S5: only the third idea, with S1's rhythm returning as texture.
Idea: each section grows from a fragment of the one before; there is no return. Suits: cinematic, drifting, dreamlike.

### Add-ons for any form
- **Half-time switch**: at a section boundary the drums keep their notes but read at half speed for 8 or 16 bars.
- **Staircase of lengths**: sections of 16, 12, 8, 4, 2, 1 bars compress before a long release.

## Choosing the form

The form is chosen in Frame, not in Arrange, because it shapes the palette (F5 needs two palettes, F8 a second meter, F3 a single ostinato). Write it in the brief section and again as the first line of the arrangement section: `Form: F<n> <name>`.

Apply these rules in this order; a later rule never overrides an earlier one:

1. **The brief.** A form, a length or a structure the brief states wins. Keep the brief's length and fit the chosen map to it.
2. **No repeat.** The form differs from the forms of the two previous runs. Read the `Form:` line of the five latest sheets you can reach (in Faulk, the `fiche-*.md` files of the five latest `.qa/po-run-*` folders, or the sheets inside the journal comments of #362): the two latest for this rule, all five for rule 5. A sheet without a `Form:` line was written before this rule and counts as F0.
3. **The profile.** When a style profile is in use, prefer the forms it names, among those rule 2 leaves; if it names none that are left, take another and record the departure from the profile.
4. **The mood.** When the brief states a mood, prefer the forms whose "Suits" line matches it.
5. **Ration F0.** F0 at most once in five runs: if one of the four previous sheets is F0, skip it; skip it too when the brief says nothing about form.
6. **Fallback.** If several forms remain, take the one whose idea serves the material best and say why in a line. If rules 3–5 leave nothing, drop rule 5, then rule 4, then rule 3, in that order; rules 1 and 2 are never dropped. With no history at all, take the form whose number is the day of the month modulo 12.
