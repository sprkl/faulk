# Style profiles

A style profile is a frame you can borrow when the brief asks for it. It holds a catalog of attributes distilled from a body of work, the moves that body favours and the ones it avoids. It never transcribes a track and never names the tracks it comes from. A profile is named after the artist only when that artist asked for it (`morzak` is the founder's own artist name, at their request). Two producers reading the same profile still make two different tracks.

## When to use one

Only when the brief explicitly asks for a profile, for example "use the morzak style" or "style: morzak". A profile name quoted from an earlier report does not count as a request. Without a request, ignore this folder: the default is your own judgement. If the brief asks for a profile that is not listed below, say so in the report and work without it.

A profile is a starting frame, not a cage. Record in the sheet every place where you depart from it, and why.

## Precedence

1. **The brief's explicit choices win**, such as a tempo, a key or a length. Note each clash with the profile as a departure.
2. **The profile comes next.**
3. **A reference track named in the same brief** adds its catalog lines only for the parameters the profile leaves open.

## How to apply it

1. **Frame**:
   - Read the whole profile. Its catalog fills the "Catalog of attributes" section of the sheet: copy the lines you keep, adapted to this track.
   - Pick tempo, mode and form from its ranges, and state each choice. Where the profile gives a half-time reading, choose one reading and say which.
   - Take two of its "moves to try". They count inside the skill's two or three arbitrary constraints, so you add at most one of your own.
   - Name the profile in the brief section of the sheet.
2. **Seed and Grow**: the harmony and rhythm lines guide the material. The track contains the profile's "signature" at least once, placed on purpose.
3. **Arrange and Finish**: check the form against the profile's form line, and the finishing notes against its mix line. Its blind spots are already addressed by the moves.
4. **Report**: list what you kept from the profile, what you bent and what you dropped.

## Format of a profile

```
# <profile-name>
One paragraph: the character in plain words, and what the profile was distilled from (counted, not named).
## Catalog         tempo · mode and harmony · sound · rhythm · melody · form · mix (measured where possible)
## Signature       the one or two moves that make it recognisable
## Moves to try    prescriptions, not measurements: constraints that keep the character and fix its blind spots
## Avoid           what would break the character
## Confidence      what was measured, what was estimated, what is unknown or chosen
```
