# Style profiles

A style profile is a frame you can borrow when the brief asks for it: a catalog of attributes distilled from a body of work, plus the moves that body favours and the ones it avoids. It never transcribes a track and never names the artist or the tracks it comes from (the skill names no source). Two producers reading the same profile still make two different tracks.

## When to use one

Only when the brief names a profile ("use the modal-dusk style", "style: modal-dusk"). Without that, ignore this folder: the default is your own judgement. A profile is a starting frame, not a cage: keep at least one of the brief's own constraints alongside it, and record in the sheet every place you depart from the profile and why.

## How to apply it

1. **Frame**: read the whole profile. Its catalog replaces the "Catalog of attributes" section of the sheet (copy the lines you keep, adapted to this track). Pick tempo, mode and form from its ranges. Take two of its "moves to try" as your arbitrary constraints, and at most one more of your own.
2. **Seed and Grow**: the harmony and rhythm lines guide the material; the "signature" line is something the track should contain at least once, placed on purpose.
3. **Arrange and Finish**: check the form against the profile's form line, and the finishing notes against its mix line, including its known blind spots, which you correct unless the brief says otherwise.
4. **Report**: name the profile in the brief section and list what you kept, what you bent, and what you dropped.

## Format of a profile

```
# <profile-name>
One paragraph: the character in plain words, and what the profile was distilled from (counted, never named).
## Catalog         tempo · mode and harmony · sound · rhythm · melody · form · mix (one or a few concrete lines each)
## Signature       the one or two moves that make it recognisable
## Moves to try    constraints that keep the character and fix its blind spots
## Avoid           what would break the character
## Confidence      what was measured, what was estimated, what is unknown
```
