# drifting-cycles

Melodic electronic music whose harmony travels: each section has its own chord picture, a bass that sometimes holds against the chords, one late lift, and a melody that is re-harmonised rather than rewritten. The colour comes from extended, suspended and borrowed chords, not from long functional progressions. Distilled from the harmonic habits of two melodic electronic producers (counted, not named), from knowledge of their work, not from measurement.

## Catalog

- **Tempo**: three readings. Half-time 68–80 bpm (felt as 136–160), mid 100–112 bpm, dance 118–126 bpm. State which reading the track uses.
- **Mode and harmony**:
  - a major or minor home, with the parallel or relative key visited in the middle;
  - cycles of 3 to 6 chords, one or two bars each, one new cycle per section of 16 or 32 bars;
  - extended chords by default: maj7, m7, add9, sus2, sus4;
  - one pedal section (the tonic held in the bass under moving chords);
  - one late lift up a whole step or a minor third.
- **Sound**: a detuned stacked lead or pitched chops, a plucked or arpeggiated layer, a warm pad, a clean sub that can hold a pedal, pumping from a sidechain, filtered noise risers.
- **Rhythm**: a half-time snare or a four-on-the-floor; a stutter or a roll only before a section change; swing on the hats only.
- **Melody**: a short hook of 4 to 8 notes stated early, varied by the chords under it rather than by its notes; one long singing phrase in the last third.
- **Form**: by sections, with a chord-less intro; favourite forms in [forms.md](../forms.md): F4 theme and variations, F9 false ending, F10 rondo, F1 reverse arc. The rotation rule there applies.
- **Mix**: full low mids, a clean sub, a present top (3–8 kHz), wide pads, bass and lead centred.

## Signature

- A bass that holds one note under moving chords for a section, then moves on the first chord of the next.
- The same melody twice, with different chords under it.

## Moves to try

1. A new cycle per section, keeping one chord of the previous cycle as an anchor.
2. A late lift (+2 or +3 semitones) at 65–75 %, with a bar thinned before it and one note held across the cut.
3. A pedal under moving chords in the build; the bass moves only on the peak's first beat.
4. A sus chord held through a phrase and resolved only at the peak.
5. The melody re-harmonised at its second statement.
6. A chord-less intro of 8 to 16 bars, arpeggio and bass only.

## Avoid

- One cycle held for the whole track.
- A key change made by transposing everything, with no thinned bar and no common note.
- More than one key change in a track under six minutes.
- Chords changing every bar in every section: keep a contrast between slow and fast harmony.
- Parts entering one by one as the only form.

## Confidence

- **Estimated from knowledge, not measured**: every line of the catalog. Check the tempos and the cycle lengths on audio (the `audio-analysis` skill, where installed) before treating them as ranges.
- **Chosen by the lead**: the favourite forms, the six moves and the Avoid list.
